[s118] EB-5 visa FAQs
[s119] Eligibility and visa availability
[s120] What is EB-5?
[s121] It is open to foreign investors who meet the requirements for a qualifying investment, a lawful source of funds, and job creation in the United States
[s122] Once the petition is approved and a visa is available, the investor, their spouse, and unmarried children under 21 may receive conditional U.S. green cards
[s123] What is the current EB-5 minimum investment?
[s124] US$800,000 for projects in a Targeted Employment Area (TEA), which covers rural areas and high-unemployment areas
[s125] US$1,050,000 for projects outside a TEA
[s126] Investors should confirm the required investment amount at the time of filing, as laws and guidance may change.
[s127] What is a TEA?
[s128] Under the RIA, TEAs fall into 2 main groups: rural areas and high-unemployment areas
[s129] Projects in a TEA may qualify for the US$800,000 investment level, and some categories also have reserved (set-aside) visas
[s130] What are the advantages of a rural EB-5 project?
[s131] Priority processing of Form I-526E by USCIS under current rules
[s132] Access to a dedicated category of set-aside visas
[s133] "Priority processing" does not mean a fixed approval timeline. Investors still need to conduct due diligence on the project, their source of funds, and visa availability under the Visa Bulletin at the time of filing.
[s134] What are the risks of an EB-5 investment?
[s135] The 3 main risks of an EB-5 investment:
[s136] A regional center lacking experience in due diligence and project selection
[s137] A project that fails to create enough jobs or misuses investor capital
[s138] A source of funds that cannot be documented to U.S. immigration authorities' requirements
[s139] Choosing an inexperienced immigration attorney or immigration firm also increases the risk to your petition.
[s140] How can you manage risk in an EB-5 investment?
[s141] A well-managed EB-5 investment aims to maximize your chances of obtaining a green card while minimizing risk to your capital. Consider 3 factors:
[s142] A regional center with a track record of successful projects
[s143] An immigration attorney with extensive hands-on experience
[s144] A transparent advisory firm that conducts independent project due diligence
[s145] What is the age limit for dependent children?
[s146] Children under 21 can be included in the application
[s147] A child's age is locked in when Form I-526 is filed
[s148] While Form I-526 is pending, the child's age does not increase
[s149] From the Form I-526 decision until the visa is issued, the child's age continues to count
[s150] Do I need to move to the U.S. right after receiving my visa?
[s151] Enter the U.S. while your visa is valid
[s152] An immigrant visa is generally valid for approximately 6 months from the date of the medical exam
[s153] You must travel to the U.S. before the visa expires to activate it
[s154] You will receive your green card approximately 3 weeks to 1 month after entry
[s155] How long must I stay in the U.S. after receiving my green card?
[s156] There is no fixed residence requirement
[s157] You need to show your intent to maintain permanent resident status
[s158] Recommendation: avoid staying outside the U.S. for more than 6 months a year
[s159] If you need to travel frequently, apply for a reentry permit
[s160] What does "current" mean in the Visa Bulletin?
[s161] A "C" ("current") means visas are available for eligible applicants in that category as of the bulletin's publication
[s162] It does not mean your petition is certain to be approved or that a visa will be issued immediately
[s163] Your petition must still be approved by USCIS, and you must complete National Visa Center (NVC) processing and your interview, or adjustment of status if eligible
[s164] Is there an EB-5 visa backlog?
[s165] According to the June 2026 Visa Bulletin, the reserved EB-5 categories (rural, high-unemployment, and infrastructure projects) currently have no backlog for applicants from all countries.
[s166] Availability in the unreserved category varies by country of birth; check the latest Visa Bulletin for your country.
[s167] The Visa Bulletin changes monthly, so this information should be reviewed regularly
[s168] Updated: June 2026.
[s169] Does EB-5 require English proficiency, a formal education, or business experience?
[s170] EB-5 does not require investors to hold a specific degree, speak English, have U.S. business experience, or have an employer sponsor
[s171] The petition focuses on a qualifying investment, a lawful source of funds, a project that creates enough jobs, and compliance with the relevant immigration requirements
[s172] Do I need to start or run a business in the U.S. myself?
[s173] If you invest through a regional center, you generally do not need to manage the business directly
[s174] Day-to-day operations are the responsibility of the project developer and the relevant management entity
[s175] You still need to understand the project's structure, your rights and obligations, the risks, and the expected timeline for return of capital as set out in the offering documents

[s003] What is the Nova Scotia Entrepreneur Stream?
[s004] A Canadian permanent residence pathway for business owners and senior managers who want to invest in, start, or acquire a business in Nova Scotia.
[s005] You must run the business in the province yourself to qualify for a provincial nomination and, from there, Canadian permanent residence.
[s006] Your spouse and your unmarried children under 22 can be included in the same application.

[s034] Principal applicant requirements
[s035] Has, within the past 10 years, owned and actively managed a business for at least 3 years, or held a senior management role for at least 5 years.

[s037] Investment requirements
[s038] Invests at least C$150,000 in a business in the Halifax area.
[s039] Invests at least C$100,000 in a business outside the Halifax area.
[s040] Presents a viable business plan that delivers economic benefit to the province.
[s041] Operates the business for at least 12 months to qualify to apply for permanent residence.
[s042] Creates at least 1 full-time job for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident when starting a new business; maintains the existing jobs when acquiring an established business.

[s044] Assessment and case preparation

[s047] You travel to Nova Scotia for an exploratory visit if you are acquiring an existing business.

[s053] You attend an interview with the Government of Nova Scotia, in person or online as requested.

[s055] When your application is approved, you sign the business performance agreement with the province and apply for your work permit.

[s057] You receive your work permit, move to Nova Scotia, make the investment, and run the business.

[s060] Apply for permanent residence
[s061] Once you are nominated, you file your permanent residence application with the Canadian federal government.

[s084] Frequently asked questions about Nova Scotia
[s085] Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?
[s086] Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?
[s087] No. A work permit gives you temporary permission to live and work in Canada. You must complete your investment, business operating and residency commitments before you can apply for permanent residence.
[s088] Do I have to run the business myself?
[s089] Do I have to run the business myself?
[s090] Yes. You must take part directly in managing and operating the business in Nova Scotia. An advisory firm can help with the business plan, but it cannot run the business on your behalf.
[s091] Is an exploratory visit to Nova Scotia mandatory?
[s092] Is an exploratory visit to Nova Scotia mandatory?
[s093] An exploratory visit is required if you are acquiring an existing business. It is an important step for assessing the business model and preparing your investment plan.
[s094] Does my family have to live in Nova Scotia?
[s095] Does my family have to live in Nova Scotia?
[s096] Before you hold permanent residence, you and your family are expected to settle in the province you applied to and to honor your residence and business commitments.
[s097] Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence?
[s098] Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence?
[s099] Once you hold Canadian permanent residence, you and your family are free to live and work in any province.
[s100] What are the main risks of the program?
[s101] What are the main risks of the program?
[s102] Risk usually comes from a business plan that is not viable, failing to operate the business as committed, failing to create or maintain the required jobs, or weak documentation of your finances.
[s103] Who is Nova Scotia right for?
[s104] Who is Nova Scotia right for?
[s105] It suits experienced business owners and senior managers who want to run a business in Canada themselves, at an investment level that is more accessible than in many of Canada's larger provinces.

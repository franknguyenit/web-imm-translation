Malta Golden Visa

What is the Malta Golden Visa?

  • The Malta Golden Visa — officially the Malta Permanent Residence Programme (MPRP) — is a Government of Malta program that grants permanent residence in Malta.
  • It is open to investors from outside the EU, EEA, and Switzerland, and to their families, through a qualifying investment.
  • You can live in Malta and travel in the Schengen Area on short stays under the 90/180-day rule.
  • Up to 4 generations of one family: your spouse, children under 29 who are unmarried and financially dependent, and the parents and grandparents on both sides.
  • Buy property from €375,000 or rent from €14,000 a year, together with the contributions set by the program.

Benefits of Maltese permanent residence

Live and build your future in Malta

  • The whole family can live, study, and work in Malta.

Permanent residence in Malta

  • Your status as a permanent resident does not expire, and it can open access to healthcare and to an advanced European education system taught in English.

Visa-free travel in the Schengen Area

  • Travel across the 29 Schengen countries under the standard short-stay rule: 90 days in any 180-day period.

A route toward Maltese citizenship

  • Once you have completed a period of lawful residence and met the residence and language conditions set by Maltese law, you may apply for naturalization.

Advantages of the Malta Golden Visa

No residence requirement

  • You are not required to live in Malta to keep your permanent residence.

Straightforward eligibility

  • There is no upper age limit, and no qualification, work experience, or English-language requirement.

Four generations of one family

  • Each dependent over 18 requires an additional contribution.
  • Your spouse and minor children can be included with no additional contribution.

Invest after you are approved

  • You make the investment only once your application has received pre-approval.

Malta Golden Visa requirements

Investment requirements

  • Buy property from €375,000, or
  • Rent property from €14,000 a year.
  • Hold the property or the lease for at least 5 years.

Contributions set by the program

  • Government contribution, non-refundable: €37,000.
  • Administrative fee: €60,000.
  • Philanthropic donation in Malta: €2,000.
  • Each dependent over 18 other than your spouse: €7,500 per person.

Proof of assets

  • At least €500,000, of which €150,000 in liquid assets, or
  • €650,000, of which €75,000 in liquid assets.

Requirements for the applicant and dependents

  • Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.
  • You pass the security background and health checks.
  • You document a lawful source of funds.
  • Dependents: financially dependent on the applicant.
  • Every family member must hold health insurance for 5 years.

Malta Golden Visa FAQs

What are the investment risks?

The two main risks

  • Choosing a developer or project without a track record, or a property that does not qualify.
  • Investment funds from an unlawful source, which leads to refusal.
  • IMM Group runs due diligence on every file before any contract is signed, to address source-of-funds risk up front.

Is documenting my source of funds complicated?

Typically simpler than the United States, Australia, or Canada

  • You document 2 layers: the source of the funds you are investing, and the source of wealth behind them.
  • For funds from a property sale, for example, you will need to show the year of purchase and the value at the time.
  • Every family documents this differently — contact IMM Group for advice on your own situation.

What does the investment consist of?

Choose one of two property routes: buy or rent

  • Buy property from €375,000, or rent from €14,000 a year, held for 5 years.
  • A government contribution of €37,000, an administrative fee of €60,000, and a €2,000 philanthropic donation.
  • Proof of assets from €500,000, of which €150,000 in liquid assets; or €650,000, of which €75,000 in liquid assets.

How long must the property be held, and when can I sell?

Held for at least 5 years

  • You commit to holding the property or the lease for at least 5 years; you are not required to file periodic proof during that time.
  • After 5 years you may sell the property or end the lease without affecting your permanent residence.

How many days a year must I spend in Malta?

No minimum stay is required to keep your permanent residence

  • You are not required to live in Malta to hold or renew your permanent residence.
  • If you are aiming at Maltese citizenship, you will need to meet the actual residence requirements set by law — see the citizenship section.

What are the requirements for the main applicant?

Basic conditions

  • Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.
  • No qualifications, formal education, English-language ability, or management experience is required.
  • You meet the background security and health requirements, and document a lawful source of funds.

Which family members can be included?

A multigenerational family

  • Your legal spouse.
  • Children, including adopted children, under 29 who are unmarried and financially dependent.
  • The parents and grandparents of both spouses, where they are financially dependent.

Are adopted children and stepchildren eligible?

Adopted children and stepchildren under 29 are eligible where they are unmarried and financially dependent

  • Adopted child: a legal certificate is required.
  • Stepchild: a legal certificate plus a notarized letter of consent from the spouse.
  • IMM Group assists with preparing the documents.

How long is Maltese permanent residence valid, and how is it renewed?

A 5-year card, renewable periodically

  • The residence card is valid for 5 years and can be renewed as long as you continue to meet the conditions.
  • After 6 years of permanent residence, you may be considered for a Maltese citizenship application if you meet the conditions.

Can my spouse work in Malta?

Yes, by applying for a work permit

  • With Maltese permanent residence, your spouse can apply for a work permit in order to work in Malta.

Do I get free movement in the Schengen Area and the EU?

Short stays in the Schengen Area — not a right to live or work elsewhere in the EU

  • Visa-free travel across the 29 Schengen countries under the short-stay rule: 90 days in any 180-day period.
  • Travel to EU countries outside the Schengen Area still requires a visitor visa under each country's rules.
  • The right to live, work, and study in any EU country arises only once you hold Maltese citizenship.

What is the difference between Schengen and the EU?

Schengen is not the same as the EU

  • The EU — the European Union: an intergovernmental organization of 27 countries.
  • Schengen: a border-free travel area, currently 29 countries.
  • Some EU countries are not in Schengen, and the reverse is also true. Malta is a member of both.

What are the requirements for Maltese citizenship?

Naturalization requirements

  • Aged 18 or older, and resident in Malta for the 12 months immediately before you apply.
  • Cumulative residence of 4 years within the 6 years before that 12-month period.
  • You meet the English or Maltese language requirement, and have no criminal record in Malta or abroad.

How long does a citizenship application take?

A long-term route

  • Naturalization can take many years — as a guide, 10–15 years — depending on your file and the rules in force.
  • While you wait, your family keeps the residence card and with it the right to live in Malta.

What does Maltese citizenship give you?

Full EU citizenship rights.

  • Freedom to live, work, and study in any EU member state.

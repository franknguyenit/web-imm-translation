[s076] What are the investment risks?

[s078] Choosing a developer or project without a track record, or a property that does not qualify.
[s079] Investment funds from an unlawful source, which leads to refusal.
[s080] IMM Group runs due diligence on every file before signing, to address source-of-funds risk up front.
[s081] Is documenting my source of funds complicated?
[s082] Typically simpler than the United States, Australia, or Canada
[s083] Proof works in 2 layers: the source of funds you are investing, and the source of wealth behind it.
[s084] For funds from a property sale, for example, you will need to show the year of purchase and the value at the time.
[s085] Every family documents this differently — contact IMM Group for advice on your own situation.
[s086] What does the investment consist of?
[s087|#bo-qua-so: nguồn viết "Chọn 1 phương án"; bản Anh dùng "one of two" đúng cách nói bản xứ] Choose one of two property routes: buy or rent

[s091] How long must the property be held, and when can I sell?
[s092] Held for at least 5 years
[s093] You commit to holding the property, or the lease, for at least 5 years; no periodic filings are required during that time to prove it.
[s094] After 5 years you may sell the property or end the lease without affecting your permanent residence.
[s095] How many days a year must I spend in Malta?
[s096] No residence requirement to keep permanent residence
[s097] You are not required to live in Malta to hold or renew your permanent residence.
[s098] If you are aiming at Maltese citizenship, you will need to meet the actual residence requirements set by law — see the citizenship section.
[s099] What must the applicant meet?
[s100] Basic conditions
[s101] Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.
[s102] No qualifications, education, English, or management experience are required.
[s103] You meet the background security and health requirements, and document a lawful source of funds.
[s104] Which family members can be included?
[s105] A multigenerational family
[s106] Your legal spouse.
[s107] Children, including adopted children, under 29 who are unmarried and financially dependent.
[s108] The parents and grandparents of both spouses, where they are financially dependent.
[s109] Are adopted children and stepchildren eligible?
[s110] Adopted children and stepchildren under 29 are eligible where they are unmarried and financially dependent
[s111] Adopted child: a legal certificate is required.
[s112] Stepchild: a legal certificate plus a notarized letter of consent from the spouse.
[s113] IMM Group assists with preparing the documents.
[s114] How long is Maltese permanent residence valid, and how is it renewed?
[s115] A 5-year card, renewed periodically
[s116] The residence card runs for 5 years and is renewed for as long as you continue to meet the conditions.
[s117] After 6 years of permanent residence, you can be assessed for Maltese citizenship if you qualify.
[s118] Can my spouse work in Malta?
[s119] Your spouse can apply for a work permit
[s120] With Maltese permanent residence, your spouse can apply for a work permit and take up employment in Malta.
[s121] Do I get free movement in the Schengen Area and the EU?
[s122] Short stays in the Schengen Area — not a right to live or work elsewhere in the EU
[s123] Visa-free travel across the 29 Schengen countries under the short-stay rule: 90 days in any 180-day period.
[s124] Travel to EU countries outside the Schengen Area still requires a visitor visa under each country's rules.
[s125] The right to live, work, and study in any EU country arises only once you hold Maltese citizenship.
[s126] What is the difference between Schengen and the EU?
[s127] Schengen is not the same as the EU
[s128] The EU — the European Union: an intergovernmental organization of 27 countries.
[s129] Schengen: a free-movement area, currently 29 countries.
[s130] Some EU countries are not in Schengen, and the reverse is also true. Malta is a member of both.
[s131] What are the requirements for Maltese citizenship?
[s132] Naturalization requirements
[s133] Aged 18 or older, and resident in Malta for the 12 months immediately before you apply.
[s134] Cumulative residence of 4 years within the 6 years before that 12-month period.
[s135] You meet the English or Maltese language requirement, and have no criminal record in Malta or abroad.
[s136] How long does a citizenship application take?
[s137] A long-term route
[s138] Naturalization can take many years — as a guide, 10–15 years — depending on your file and the rules in force.
[s139] While you wait, the family keeps the residence card and with it the right to live in Malta.
[s140] What does Maltese citizenship give you?
[s141] Full EU citizenship rights.
[s142] Freedom to live, work, and study in any EU member state.

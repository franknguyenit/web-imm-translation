[s003] What is Montenegro citizenship by investment?
[s004] The Montenegro citizenship by investment program grants citizenship of Montenegro — a European country and NATO member — to non-EU investors and their families.
[s005] Applications are made through a government donation combined with an investment in a designated real estate project.
[s006] You and your family receive Montenegrin citizenship for life if your application is approved.
[s007] Montenegrin citizenship opens a route to living in the EU if and when Montenegro joins the European Union.
[s008] Once you hold citizenship and have lived in Montenegro for at least 3 continuous years, you may qualify to apply for a US E-2 visa.
[s009] **Who you can include:** your spouse, children under 18, and children over 18 who are unmarried and financially dependent.

[s084] Property holding period
[s085] The program sets no minimum holding period for the property, and citizenship is held for life once granted.

[s088] What happens to my citizenship if the project fails?
[s089] How project risk affects citizenship
[s090] Under the structure of the program, citizenship is tied to an eligible application and an investment that has been made; construction risk is mitigated by the deposit the developer places with the government.
[s091] Even so, this remains a real estate investment that carries risk — IMM Group reviews each project closely before recommending it.
[s092] Is there a cap on applications?
[s093] Program status
[s094] The program was capped by total number of applications and ran for a fixed period; it has been closed to new applications since the end of 2022.

[s096] What benefits and visa-free travel do citizens get?
[s097] Rights of Montenegrin citizens
[s098] Montenegrin citizens have access to public education and healthcare: children attend state schools free of charge through the end of secondary school, and health insurance covers treatment at public hospitals.
[s099] The passport is among the stronger ones, with visa-free or eVisa access to a wide range of countries (the number changes over time).
[s100] Has Montenegro joined the EU?
[s101] EU accession process
[s102] Montenegro is not an EU member yet. Accession talks are under way and no date has been set; target dates have moved before.
[s103] The right to live and work across the EU would arise only if and when Montenegro formally joins. (Updated: June 2026.)
[s104] Is there a US E-2 treaty with Montenegro?
[s105] The basis for looking at the E-2 visa
[s106] The treaty of commerce and navigation between the United States and Montenegro is what allows Montenegrin citizens to consider the E-2 visa.
[s107] Once you hold citizenship and have 3 or more continuous years of residence, you can be assessed against the E-2 rules in force when you apply. Any changes depend on the policy of both countries.
[s108] Is there a residence requirement?
[s109] Residence requirements
[s110] The program sets no residence requirement for obtaining or keeping citizenship.
[s111] Note: if you plan to apply for a US E-2 visa later, the E-2 rules set their own requirement for continuous residence in Montenegro.

[s116] What must the applicant meet?

[s120] Can parents be included?
[s121] Who counts as a dependent
[s122] Unmarried, financially dependent children up to age 28 can be included in the application.
[s123] Parents cannot be included in the same application.
[s124] If you need a route that covers parents as well, you could look at another program — for example the Portugal Golden Visa, which covers several generations — or a separate Montenegro application for them.

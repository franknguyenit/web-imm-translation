Benefits of Grenadian citizenship

Access to the US E-2 visa

  • The E-2 is a long-term US investor and business visa that can be extended without limit while the business continues to operate and you continue to qualify; it is a non-immigrant visa, not a green card.
  • You become eligible to file an E-2 application after at least 3 continuous years of domicile in Grenada — a stricter test than simply holding the passport

What does the real estate route actually cost?

  • An equity investment from US$270,000 in a government-approved project, plus a US$50,000 government contribution.

What happens to my citizenship if the project fails?

Once granted, your citizenship does not depend on the outcome of the project

How long it takes to obtain citizenship

  • Assessment takes 6–8 months

Investor protection if a project fails

  • The failure of a project is not a ground for withdrawing citizenship that has already been granted
  • Whether you can recover your capital if a project is not completed depends on the terms of your purchase agreement with the developer, so review those terms carefully before you sign
  • Recovering your capital once the holding requirement has been met does not affect your citizenship

Do I need to have lived in Grenada to apply for an E-2 visa?

The E-2 visa requires 3 years of domicile in Grenada

  • Grenadian citizens who acquired citizenship through investment must be domiciled in Grenada for 3 continuous years before filing an E-2 application — a US requirement that applies to citizenship obtained through an investment program. Domicile means actual residence with the intention of remaining, which is a stricter test than holding the passport.
  • It is the only route to the E-2 visa in the Caribbean: Grenada is the only Caribbean country with an E-2 treaty with the US, in force since 1989.

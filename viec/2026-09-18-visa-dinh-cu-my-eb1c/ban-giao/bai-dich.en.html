EB-1C Green Card for Multinational Executives and Managers

What is the EB-1C green card?

  • The EB-1C is a U.S. immigrant visa that grants an unconditional green card to multinational managers and senior executives.
  • It is designed for senior leaders transferring from a company abroad to run its U.S. subsidiary, branch, or affiliate.
  • Your spouse and children under 21 receive green cards alongside you.
  • Unlike EB-5, an EB-1C green card does not carry a 2-year conditional period.
  • After at least 5 years as permanent residents, and once you meet the requirements, you and your family may apply for U.S. citizenship.

EB-1C green card benefits

Green cards for your whole family

  • You, your spouse, and your children under 21 all receive U.S. green cards.

Live and work in the U.S.

  • Your whole family can live, study, and work anywhere in the U.S. as permanent residents.

U.S. schooling for your children

  • Your children can attend U.S. public schools on the same basis as other permanent residents.

Permanent resident benefits

  • Your family can access the healthcare, education, and social benefits available to permanent residents; only rights reserved for U.S. citizens are excluded.

A path to U.S. citizenship

  • After at least 5 years as green card holders, and once you meet the requirements, you and your family may apply for naturalization.

Advantages of the EB-1C visa

An unconditional green card

  • No 2-year conditional green card period, unlike EB-5.

No fixed investment amount

  • Unlike EB-5, EB-1C does not require a fixed investment amount, but you must meet the business and managerial-role requirements.

Premium processing available

  • Form I-140 petitions are eligible for premium processing by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). Total time to a green card depends on your case and the Visa Bulletin.

Hands-on leadership of your U.S. business

  • You manage and run the U.S. subsidiary yourself.

A path to citizenship

  • Once you become a permanent resident and meet the residence, background, and English language requirements, you may apply for U.S. citizenship.

Suited to companies already operating in the U.S.

  • Designed for business owners whose U.S. company has been operating for at least 1 year and who need to transfer a senior leader to run it.

EB-1C visa requirements

Company requirements

  • The parent company outside the U.S. has the scale and financial capacity to invest in the U.S.
  • The U.S. subsidiary has been operating effectively for at least 1 year and has a viable business plan.
  • The companies meet the same criteria as for L-1A: a qualifying parent–subsidiary relationship and a managerial position.

Applicant requirements

  • Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.
  • Currently holds a managerial or senior executive position at the parent company abroad.
  • Has the capability and experience to run the U.S. subsidiary.
  • Meets background security and medical requirements.

The EB-1C application process

Establish a U.S. subsidiary

Set up the legal entity, ownership structure, and business operations in the U.S.

Operate the company for at least 1 year

The U.S. subsidiary must be genuinely operating, performing well, and following a growth plan.

EB-1C eligibility assessment

We assess the company's eligibility, the applicant's role, and the ability to meet EB-1C requirements.

Prepare and file with USCIS

Prepare the corporate and applicant documents and file Form I-140 with USCIS.

Consular interview or green card processing in the U.S.

Depending on your case, you attend a consular interview abroad or apply for your green card inside the U.S. through adjustment of status.

EB-1C green card FAQs

Is EB-1C an immigrant visa?

What EB-1C is

  • Yes. EB-1C is an immigrant visa category that grants a U.S. green card to managers and senior executives of multinational companies.

How is EB-1C different from L-1A?

EB-1C vs. L-1A

  • L-1A is a temporary, nonimmigrant work visa. EB-1C is an immigrant visa that grants a green card if you meet the requirements.

How is EB-1C different from EB-5?

EB-1C vs. EB-5

  • EB-5 requires a fixed investment in a qualifying project. EB-1C has no fixed investment amount, but requires a U.S. company that has been operating for at least 1 year and needs a senior leader to run it.

Is the EB-1C green card conditional?

An unconditional green card

  • No. EB-1C grants an unconditional green card, so you do not need to file a petition to remove conditions, as EB-5 investors must.

Can my family come with me?

Family members

  • Yes. Your spouse and children under 21 may receive green cards alongside you.

How long must the U.S. company have been operating?

Operating history of the U.S. company

  • The U.S. subsidiary must have been operating effectively for at least 1 year before you begin the EB-1C application.

Can I apply for U.S. citizenship after EB-1C?

A path to citizenship

  • Yes, potentially. After at least 5 years as permanent residents, and once you meet the naturalization requirements, you and your family may apply for U.S. citizenship.

What are the main risks of EB-1C?

Key risks

  • The main risk lies in proving that the business is genuine and operating effectively, that the applicant holds a true senior managerial role, and that the U.S. company has a real need for that role.

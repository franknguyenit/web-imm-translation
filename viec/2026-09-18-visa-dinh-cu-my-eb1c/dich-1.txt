[s003] What is the EB-1C green card?
[s004] The EB-1C is a U.S. immigrant visa that grants an unconditional green card to multinational managers and senior executives.
[s005] It is designed for senior leaders transferring from a company abroad to run its U.S. subsidiary, branch, or affiliate.
[s006] Your spouse and children under 21 receive green cards alongside you.
[s007] Unlike EB-5, an EB-1C green card does not go through a 2-year conditional period.
[s008] After at least 5 years as permanent residents, and once they meet the requirements, family members may apply for U.S. citizenship.

[s010] EB-1C green card benefits
[s011] Green cards for your whole family
[s012] You, your spouse, and your children under 21 all receive U.S. green cards.
[s013] Live and work in the U.S.
[s014] Your whole family can live, study, and work anywhere in the U.S. as permanent residents.
[s015] U.S. schooling for your children
[s016] Your children can attend U.S. public schools on the same basis as other permanent residents.
[s017] Permanent resident benefits
[s018] Your family can access healthcare, education, and social benefits available to permanent residents, except rights reserved for U.S. citizens.
[s019] A path to U.S. citizenship
[s020] After at least 5 years as green card holders, and once they meet the requirements, family members may apply for naturalization.
[s021] Advantages of the EB-1C visa
[s022] An unconditional green card
[s023] No 2-year conditional green card period, unlike EB-5.
[s024] No fixed investment amount
[s025] EB-1C does not require a fixed investment amount as EB-5 does, but you must meet the business and managerial-role requirements.
[s026] Competitive processing times
[s027] Form I-140 petitions are eligible for USCIS premium processing. Total time to a green card depends on your case and the visa bulletin.
[s028] Hands-on leadership of your U.S. business
[s029] You directly manage or run the U.S. subsidiary yourself.
[s030] A path to citizenship
[s031] Once you become a permanent resident and meet the residence, background, and English language requirements, you may apply for U.S. citizenship.
[s032] Suited to companies already operating in the U.S.
[s033] Designed for business owners whose U.S. company has been operating for at least 1 year and who need to transfer a senior leader to run it.
[s034] EB-1C visa requirements
[s035] Company requirements
[s036] The parent company outside the U.S. has the scale and financial capacity to invest in the U.S.
[s037] The U.S. subsidiary has been operating effectively for at least 1 year and has a viable business plan.
[s038] The companies meet the L-1A criteria (a qualifying parent–subsidiary relationship and a managerial position).
[s039] Applicant requirements
[s040] Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.
[s041] Currently holds a managerial or senior executive position at the parent company abroad.
[s042] Has the capability and experience to run the U.S. subsidiary.
[s043] Meets security background and medical requirements.
[s044] The EB-1C application process
[s045] Establish a U.S. subsidiary
[s046] Set up the legal entity, ownership structure, and business operations in the U.S.
[s047] Operate the company for at least 1 year
[s048] The U.S. subsidiary must be genuinely operating, performing well, and following a growth plan.
[s049] EB-1C eligibility assessment
[s050] We assess the company's eligibility, the applicant's role, and the ability to meet EB-1C requirements.
[s051] Prepare and file with USCIS
[s052] Prepare the corporate and applicant documents and file Form I-140 with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS).
[s053] Interview or green card processing
[s054] Depending on the case, the applicant attends a consular interview or completes green card processing inside the U.S.

[s072] EB-1C green card FAQs
[s073] Is EB-1C an immigrant visa?
[s074] What EB-1C is
[s075] Yes. EB-1C is an immigrant visa category that grants U.S. permanent residence to managers and senior executives of multinational companies.
[s076] How is EB-1C different from L-1A?
[s077] EB-1C vs. L-1A
[s078] L-1A is a temporary, nonimmigrant work visa. EB-1C is an immigrant visa that grants a green card if you meet the requirements.
[s079] How is EB-1C different from EB-5?
[s080] EB-1C vs. EB-5
[s081] EB-5 requires a fixed investment in a qualifying project. EB-1C has no fixed investment amount, but requires a U.S. company that has been operating for at least 1 year and needs a senior leader to run it.
[s082] Is the EB-1C green card conditional?
[s083] An unconditional green card
[s084] No. EB-1C grants an unconditional green card, so there is no petition to remove conditions as with EB-5.
[s085] Can my family come with me?
[s086] Family members
[s087] Yes. Your spouse and children under 21 may receive green cards alongside you.
[s088] How long must the U.S. company have been operating?
[s089] Operating history of the U.S. company
[s090] The U.S. subsidiary must have been operating effectively for at least 1 year before you begin the EB-1C application.
[s091] Can I apply for U.S. citizenship after EB-1C?
[s092] A path to citizenship
[s093] Yes, potentially. After at least 5 years as permanent residents, and once they meet the naturalization requirements, family members may apply for U.S. citizenship.
[s094] What are the main risks of EB-1C?
[s095] Key risks
[s096] The main risk lies in proving that the business is genuine and operating effectively, that the applicant holds a true senior managerial role, and that the U.S. company has a real need for that role.

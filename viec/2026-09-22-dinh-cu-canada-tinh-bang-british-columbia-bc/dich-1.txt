[s003] What is BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration?
[s004] A Canadian immigration route for business owners and senior managers who want to invest in, start or acquire a business in British Columbia.
[s005] You must operate the business in BC yourself in order to qualify for a provincial nomination and move toward Canadian permanent residence.
[s006] Your spouse and children under 22 can be included in the same application.
[s007] The minimum investment starts at C$200,000.
[s008] British Columbia has a milder climate than much of Canada, sits beside the U.S. border for cross-border trade, and is home to large, well-established immigrant communities.

[s010] Benefits of immigrating to British Columbia
[s011] Your whole family moves to BC
[s012] You, your spouse and your dependent children can live, study and work in British Columbia during the work permit stage.
[s013] Your children study in Canada
[s014] Children attend public school free of charge through the end of high school; once your family holds permanent residence, they can attend university at domestic tuition rates.
[s015] A path to permanent residence
[s016] Once you have completed your investment and business operating commitments and met the province's conditions, your family can apply for Canadian permanent residence.
[s017] A route to Canadian citizenship
[s018] After obtaining permanent residence and meeting the residency, tax, language and background requirements, your family can consider applying for Canadian citizenship.
[s019] Why investors choose this program
[s020] A strong provincial economy
[s021] BC is one of Canada's most dynamic provincial economies, well placed for business and international trade.
[s022] Established immigrant communities
[s023] British Columbia is home to large, well-established immigrant communities, particularly in and around Vancouver.
[s024] No deposit required
[s025] You are not required to place a deposit with the province in order to join the program.
[s026] Investment from C$200,000
[s027] This applies to BC's base entrepreneur stream, with the investment made in an eligible business.
[s028] No requirement to be profitable immediately
[s029] The business must genuinely operate and create jobs as committed; profitability depends on your business model and the market.
[s030] At least 1 job created
[s031] The business must create at least 1 full-time job for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
[s032] Applicant requirements
[s033] You are a business owner or senior manager
[s034] At least 3 years of experience as a business owner holding at least 10% of the equity within the last 10 years; or at least 4 years of senior management experience within the last 10 years.
[s035] Total personal assets of at least C$600,000.
[s036] At least 1 year of post-secondary education, or experience owning and running a business in which you held 100% of the equity, in line with the program's conditions.
[s037] English or French at Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 4, roughly equivalent to IELTS General Training 4.0.
[s038] Meets background security and medical requirements.
[s039] Investment requirements
[s040] A minimum investment of C$200,000 in a business in BC.
[s041] Establish a new eligible business or acquire an existing one.
[s042] A viable business plan that delivers economic benefit to the province.
[s043] Hands-on operation of the business in BC.
[s044] At least 1 full-time job created for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
[s045] The BC application process
[s046] Assessment and file preparation
[s047] We assess your eligibility, assets, management experience, English level and overall fit with the program.
[s048] Exploratory visit to British Columbia
[s049] You visit BC to look at the business environment, the market and investment opportunities in the province.
[s050] Submit an Expression of Interest (EOI)
[s051] Submit an Expression of Interest to the BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration program.
[s052] Receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) and file your application
[s053] Once you are invited to apply, you complete the full application for the province.
[s054] Provincial interview
[s055] You attend an interview with the BC government if one is requested.
[s056] Sign the performance agreement and apply for a work permit
[s057] Once your application is approved, you sign the business performance agreement and apply for a work permit.
[s058] Move to Canada to invest and run the business
[s059] You receive your work permit, move to BC, make the investment, operate the business and deliver on your commitments to the province.
[s060] Submit the final report and request the provincial nomination
[s061] Once the investment, operating and residency conditions are met, you submit a final report to request the provincial nomination.
[s062] Apply for permanent residence
[s063] After you are nominated, you apply for permanent residence with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

[s086] Frequently asked questions about British Columbia
[s087] Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?
[s088] Is a work permit the same as permanent residence
[s089] No. A work permit gives you temporary permission to live and work in Canada. You must complete your investment, business operating and residency commitments before you can apply for permanent residence.
[s090] Do I have to run the business myself?
[s091] Do I have to run the business myself
[s092] Yes. You must personally manage and operate the business in BC. IMM Group and our partners can help with the business plan, but we cannot run the business on your behalf.
[s093] Is an exploratory visit to BC mandatory?
[s094] Is an exploratory visit to BC mandatory
[s095] The BC PNP encourages you to make an exploratory visit before you apply, so that you understand the market, the business opportunities and daily life in the province.
[s096] Does my family have to live in BC?
[s097] Does my family have to live in BC
[s098] Before you hold permanent residence, you and your family are expected to settle in the province you applied to. Under BC PNP guidance, you must live close to your business location and be present in BC for most of the work permit period.
[s099] Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence?
[s100] Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence
[s101] Once you hold Canadian permanent residence, your family has the right to live and work in any province.
[s102] What are the main risks of the program?
[s103] What are the main risks of the program
[s104] Risk usually comes from a business plan that is not viable, failing to operate the business as committed, not creating enough jobs, or weak documentation of your finances and management experience.
[s105] Who is BC best suited to?
[s106] Who is BC best suited to
[s107] It suits business owners and senior managers with solid financial capacity who want to run a business in a well-developed province with established immigrant communities and an environment that works for family life.

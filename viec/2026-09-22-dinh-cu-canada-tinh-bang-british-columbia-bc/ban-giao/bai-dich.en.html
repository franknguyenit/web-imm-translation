BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration British Columbia, Canada

What is BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration?

  • BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration is the Canadian immigration route, run under the British Columbia Provincial Nominee Program (PNP), for business owners and senior managers who want to invest in, start or acquire a business in British Columbia.
  • You must operate the business in British Columbia yourself to qualify for a provincial nomination and move toward Canadian permanent residence.
  • Your spouse and children under 22 can be included in the same application.
  • The minimum investment is C$200,000.
  • British Columbia offers a milder climate than most of Canada, a location well suited to trade with the U.S., and large, well-established immigrant communities.

Benefits of immigrating to British Columbia

Your whole family moves to BC

  • You, your spouse and your dependent children can live, study and work in British Columbia while you hold your work permit.

Your children study in Canada

  • Children attend public school free of charge through the end of high school; once your family holds permanent residence, they can attend university at domestic tuition rates.

A path to permanent residence

  • Once you have met your investment, business-operating and residency commitments to the province, your family can apply for Canadian permanent residence.

A route to Canadian citizenship

  • After obtaining permanent residence and meeting the residency, tax, language and background requirements, your family can consider applying for Canadian citizenship.

Why investors choose this program

A strong provincial economy

  • BC is one of Canada's most dynamic economies and is well positioned for business and international trade.

Established immigrant communities

  • British Columbia is home to large, well-established immigrant communities, particularly in and around Vancouver.

No deposit required

  • The province does not require you to place a deposit to take part in the program.

Investment from C$200,000

  • This applies to the BC PNP Base Stream, with the investment made in an eligible business.

No immediate profitability requirement

  • The business must be actively operating and create the jobs you committed to; profitability depends on your business model and the market.

Only 1 new job required

  • The business must create at least 1 full-time job for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.

Applicant requirements

You are a business owner or senior manager

  • At least 3 years of experience as a business owner holding 10% or more of the equity within the past 10 years, or at least 4 years of senior management experience within the past 10 years.
  • Personal net worth of at least C$600,000.
  • At least 1 year of post-secondary education, or experience owning and running a business in which you held 100% of the equity, as set out in the program guide.
  • English or French at Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 4, roughly equivalent to IELTS General Training 4.0.
  • Clear security, background and medical checks.

Investment requirements

  • A minimum investment of C$200,000 in a business in BC.
  • Establish a new eligible business or acquire an existing one.
  • A viable business plan that delivers economic benefit to the province.
  • Hands-on, day-to-day management of the business in BC.
  • At least 1 full-time job created for a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.

The BC application process

Assessment and case preparation

We assess your eligibility, your assets, your management experience, your English-language ability and your overall fit with the program.

Exploratory visit to British Columbia

You travel to BC to assess the business environment, the market and the investment opportunities in the province.

Submit your registration (EOI)

You submit an online registration, the BC PNP's expression of interest step, to the Entrepreneur Immigration program.

Receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) and submit your application

Once you are invited to apply, you submit your full application to the province.

Provincial interview

If the province requests one, you attend an interview with the BC PNP.

Sign the performance agreement and apply for a work permit

Once your application is approved, you sign the performance agreement with the province and apply for a work permit.

Move to BC to invest and run your business

You receive your work permit, move to BC, make your investment, operate the business and deliver on your commitments to the province.

Submit the final report and request the provincial nomination

Once you have met the investment, operating and residency conditions, you submit your final report and request the provincial nomination.

Apply for permanent residence

After you are nominated, you apply for permanent residence with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

Frequently asked questions about BC PNP Entrepreneur Immigration

Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?

Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?

  • No. A work permit gives you temporary permission to live and work in Canada. You must complete your investment, business operating and residency commitments before you can apply for permanent residence.

Do I have to run the business myself?

Do I have to run the business myself?

  • Yes. You must personally manage and operate the business in BC. IMM Group and our partners can help with the business plan, but we cannot run the business on your behalf.

Is an exploratory visit to BC mandatory?

Is an exploratory visit to BC mandatory?

  • The BC PNP encourages you to make an exploratory visit before you apply, so that you understand the market, the business opportunities and daily life in the province.

Does my family have to live in BC?

Does my family have to live in BC?

  • Before you hold permanent residence, you and your family are expected to settle in the province you applied to. Under BC PNP guidance, you must live near your business location and spend most of the work permit period in British Columbia.

Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence?

Can we live in another province once we have permanent residence?

  • Once you hold Canadian permanent residence, you and your family are free to live and work in any province.

What are the main risks of the program?

What are the main risks of the program?

  • Risk usually comes from a business plan that is not viable, failing to operate the business as committed, not creating enough jobs, or weak documentation of your finances and management experience.

Who is BC best suited to?

Who is BC best suited to?

  • It suits business owners and senior managers with sound financial capacity who want to run a business in a well-developed province with established immigrant communities and a strong environment for family life.

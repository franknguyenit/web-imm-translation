# Entrepreneur Immigration to Canada The Ontario Entrepreneur Stream

### What is the Ontario Entrepreneur Stream?

- The stream was designed for business owners and senior managers who wanted to invest in, establish or acquire a business in the province of Ontario.
- Investors had to run the Ontario business themselves to qualify for Canadian permanent residence.
- A spouse and children under 22 could be included in the same application.
- Ontario is one of Canada's major economic, political, cultural and service hubs, home to cities such as Toronto and Ottawa.
- The Ontario Entrepreneur Stream is closed to new applications. This page is kept for reference; if Canada is still your goal, our advisors can point you to the Canadian programs that are a better fit today. IMM Group's page states that the stream closed in February 2024; later updates to the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP) show that the Entrepreneur Stream was formally closed on November 4, 2024, following a pause in application intake.

## Benefits of the Ontario Entrepreneur Stream

### Relocation for the whole family

- The investor, spouse and dependent children could live, study and work in Ontario for the duration of the work permit.

### Education for your children in Canada

- Children could attend public school free of charge through the end of high school. Once the family holds permanent residence, children can attend university at domestic tuition rates.

### A route to permanent residence

- Once the investment and business commitments were met and the province's conditions satisfied, the family could apply for Canadian permanent residence.

### A path to Canadian citizenship

- Once the family holds permanent residence and has met the residency, tax, language and background requirements, they can consider applying for Canadian citizenship.

## Why investors chose this stream

### Basic education requirement

- A high school diploma was the only education requirement.

### No same-industry experience required

- Investors did not need experience in the same industry as the business they planned to run in Ontario.

### No English test at the application stage

- No English language certificate was required at the initial application stage.

### No age limit

- The stream set no maximum age for the applicant.

### No security deposit

- The province required no security deposit.

### No requirement to turn a profit immediately

- The Canadian business did not have to be profitable in its early stages, but it had to be genuinely operating and create at least 1 job for a Canadian worker.

### A province with real economic weight

- Ontario is one of Canada's most important economic, education and service centers, with strong trade connections and a large consumer market.

## Applicant requirements

### Business owner or senior manager

- At least 2 years of management experience within the previous 5 years.
- A minimum net worth of C$400,000.
- Meets background security and medical requirements.

### Investment requirements

- A minimum investment of C$200,000 in an Ontario business.
- A viable business plan that delivered economic benefit to the province.
- Hands-on operation of the business in Ontario.
- Creation of at least 1 job for a Canadian worker within 18 months.

## The Ontario application process

### Assessment and file preparation

The applicant's eligibility, net worth, management experience and fit with the stream were assessed.

### Exploratory visit to Ontario

The investor traveled to Ontario to assess the business environment and prepare an investment plan.

### Expression of Interest (EOI) submission

The Expression of Interest was submitted to the Ontario government.

### Invitation to Apply (ITA) and full application

Once invited, the investor completed the formal application for the province.

### Provincial interview

The investor attended an interview with the Ontario government.

### Work permit application

Once approved by the province, the investor applied to the federal government for a work permit.

### Move to Canada, invest and run the business

With the work permit issued, the investor moved to Ontario, made the investment and ran the business in person.

### Permanent residence application

After meeting the business and residency conditions, the investor applied for Canadian permanent residence.

## Frequently asked questions about the Ontario Entrepreneur Stream

#### Is the Ontario Entrepreneur Stream still accepting new applications?

Is the Ontario Entrepreneur Stream still accepting new applications

- No. IMM Group's page states that the stream closed in February 2024. According to later OINP updates, the Entrepreneur Stream was formally closed on November 4, 2024, after application intake had been paused since December 2023.

#### Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?

Is a work permit the same as permanent residence

- No. A work permit is temporary permission to live and work in Canada. The investor had to complete the investment, business operation and residency commitments before applying for permanent residence (PR).

#### Did the investor have to run the business personally?

Did the investor have to run the business personally

- Yes. The investor had to manage and operate the Ontario business in person. An advisory firm can help with the business plan, but it cannot run the business on the investor's behalf.

#### Did the family have to live in Ontario?

Did the family have to live in Ontario

- Before permanent residence was granted, the investor and family had to stay established in the province they applied through, living and working there in line with the commitments they made to the stream.

#### After permanent residence, can the family live in another province?

After permanent residence, can the family live in another province

- Once permanent residence is granted, the family can live and work in any province in Canada, like any other permanent resident.

#### What were the main risks of the stream?

What were the main risks of the stream

- The usual risks were a business plan that did not hold up under review, failure to meet the operating conditions, not creating enough jobs, or weak evidence of management experience and financial standing.

EB-5 Visa: A Path to a U.S. Green Card for Your Whole Family

What is the EB-5 visa program?

  • The EB-5 visa is a U.S. investment immigration program established by Congress in 1990
  • Minimum investment: US$800,000 in a Targeted Employment Area (TEA) project
  • Creation of 10 full-time jobs for U.S. workers
  • Green cards for the whole family: you, your spouse, and your unmarried children under 21
  • EB-5 investment through a regional center: a streamlined, transparent process with structured risk management

EB-5 visa FAQs

Eligibility and visa availability

What is the EB-5 visa program?

  • The program is open to foreign investors who meet the requirements for a qualifying investment, a lawful source of funds, and job creation in the United States
  • Once the petition is approved and a visa is available, the investor, their spouse, and unmarried children under 21 may receive conditional U.S. green cards

What is the current EB-5 minimum investment?

  • US$800,000 for projects in a Targeted Employment Area (TEA), which covers rural areas and high-unemployment areas
  • US$1,050,000 for projects outside a TEA

Investors should confirm the required investment amount at the time of filing, as laws and guidance may change.

What is a TEA?

  • Under the RIA, TEAs fall into 2 main groups: rural areas and high-unemployment areas
  • Projects in a TEA may qualify for the US$800,000 investment level, and some categories also have reserved (set-aside) visas

What are the advantages of a rural EB-5 project?

  • Priority processing of Form I-526E by USCIS under current rules
  • Access to a dedicated category of set-aside visas

"Priority processing" does not mean a fixed processing timeline. Investors still need to conduct due diligence on the project, their source of funds, and visa availability under the Visa Bulletin at the time of filing.

What are the risks of an EB-5 investment?

The 3 main risks of an EB-5 investment:

  • A regional center lacking experience in due diligence and project selection
  • A project that fails to create enough jobs or misuses investor capital
  • A source of funds that cannot be documented to USCIS requirements

Choosing an inexperienced immigration attorney or immigration firm also increases the risk to your petition.

How can you manage risk in an EB-5 investment?

A well-managed EB-5 investment aims to maximize your chances of obtaining a green card while minimizing risk to your capital. Consider 3 factors:

  • A regional center with a track record of successful projects
  • An immigration attorney with extensive hands-on experience
  • A transparent advisory firm that conducts independent project due diligence

What is the age limit for dependent children?

Unmarried children under 21 can be included in the application

  • A child's age is locked in when Form I-526 is filed
  • While Form I-526 is pending, the child's age does not increase
  • From the Form I-526 decision until the visa is issued, the child's age continues to count

Do I need to move to the U.S. right after receiving my visa?

Enter the U.S. while your visa is valid

  • An immigrant visa is generally valid for up to 6 months from the date of the medical exam
  • You must enter the U.S. before the visa expires to begin your permanent residence
  • Your green card typically arrives about 3 weeks to 1 month after entry

How long must I stay in the U.S. after receiving my green card?

There is no minimum-stay requirement

  • You need to show your intent to maintain permanent resident status
  • As a guideline, avoid spending more than 6 months a year outside the U.S.
  • If you need to travel frequently, apply for a reentry permit

What does "current" mean in the Visa Bulletin?

  • A "C" ("current") means visas are available for eligible applicants in that category as of the bulletin's publication
  • It does not mean your petition is certain to be approved or that a visa will be issued immediately
  • Your petition must still be approved by USCIS, and you must complete National Visa Center (NVC) processing and your interview, or adjustment of status if eligible

Is there an EB-5 visa backlog?

  • According to the June 2026 Visa Bulletin, the reserved EB-5 categories (rural, high-unemployment, and infrastructure projects) were current for applicants from all countries, with no backlog
  • Availability in the unreserved EB-5 category varies by country of birth; check the latest Visa Bulletin for your country
  • The Visa Bulletin changes monthly, so this information should be reviewed regularly

Does EB-5 require English proficiency, a formal education, or business experience?

  • EB-5 does not require investors to hold a specific degree, speak English, have U.S. business experience, or have an employer sponsor
  • The petition focuses on a qualifying investment, a lawful source of funds, a project that creates enough jobs, and compliance with the relevant immigration requirements

Do I need to start or run a business in the U.S. myself?

  • If you invest through an EB-5 regional center, you generally do not need to manage the business directly
  • Day-to-day operations are the responsibility of the project developer and the relevant management entity
  • You still need to understand the project's structure, your rights and obligations, the risks, and the expected timeline for return of capital as set out in the offering documents

Source of funds

What counts as a lawful source of funds for EB-5?

  • It is investment capital that can be shown to come from lawful sources: income, business profits, the sale of assets, dividends, inheritance, gifts, or a qualifying loan
  • USCIS looks not only at where the money came from, but also at whether the entire evidentiary record is consistent, credible, and fully documented

What is the path of funds?

  • It is the process of documenting how the money moved from its original source to the EB-5 investment account
  • For example, proceeds from a real estate sale require proof of ownership, the purchase and sale agreement, payment records, tax filings (if applicable), bank statements, and wire transfer records
  • The clearer the path of funds, the lower the risk of receiving a Request for Evidence (RFE)

Can I use gifted funds, an inheritance, real estate sale proceeds, or a loan?

  • Yes, provided both the source of funds and the path of funds are fully documented
  • Gifted or inherited funds: you need to document the donor's source of funds or the origin of the inherited assets
  • Loan proceeds: the collateral, the loan terms, and the USCIS requirements in effect at the time of filing all need to be reviewed
  • Families should have their source of funds assessed before transferring any capital

Projects and the USCIS process

How does an EB-5 regional center investment differ from a direct EB-5 investment?

  • Direct EB-5 investment: the investor creates and documents direct jobs within their own business
  • Regional center investment: the investor contributes capital (US$800,000 for a TEA project) to a project that a regional center sponsors and raises capital for, creating 10 jobs for U.S. workers
  • Better suited to families who want to immigrate but do not want to run a business in the United States themselves

What is Form I-956F?

  • Form I-956F (Application for Approval of an Investment in a Commercial Enterprise) is the application a regional center files to obtain USCIS approval of an EB-5 investment project
  • A project with a well-documented or approved Form I-956F makes it easier for investors to assess its compliance
  • Form I-956F does not replace due diligence on financial risk, job-creation risk, and the project's ability to provide a return of capital

What is Form I-526E?

  • Form I-526E (Immigrant Petition by Regional Center Investor) is the petition an EB-5 investor files when investing through a regional center project
  • The petition typically covers the investor's background, source of funds, path of funds, the investment, and the EB-5 project
  • Once Form I-526E is approved, the family proceeds with immigrant visa processing through the National Visa Center (NVC) and a U.S. consulate, or with the appropriate procedure if they are in the United States and eligible

What is Form I-829, and when is it filed?

  • Form I-829 is the petition to remove conditions on an EB-5 green card
  • The investor must file Form I-829 during the 90-day period before the 2-year conditional green card expires
  • USCIS reviews whether the investment has been sustained as required and whether the project has created enough qualifying jobs

How many jobs must an EB-5 project create?

  • Each EB-5 investor must be credited with at least 10 qualifying full-time jobs for U.S. workers under EB-5 rules
  • For regional center investments, jobs may be counted using reasonable economic methodologies — direct, indirect, and induced jobs — provided the requirements are met

What is a job cushion?

  • A job cushion is the number of projected jobs above the minimum required for all EB-5 investors in the project
  • The larger the job cushion, the greater the project's margin for meeting its job-creation target
  • It is an important criterion in project due diligence, but it does not ensure Form I-829 approval

How do I choose the right EB-5 project?

Investors should weigh 5 sets of criteria together:

  • Job-creation capacity and job cushion
  • Targeted Employment Area (TEA) status — rural area or high-unemployment area
  • Track record of the regional center and the developer
  • Capital structure, collateral, and exit strategy
  • Transparency of the legal documentation

The right project should support both your immigration goals and your approach to managing capital risk.

Family and residence

Who can be included in an EB-5 petition?

  • The principal applicant's lawful spouse and unmarried children under 21
  • If a child is close to 21, the family should review the child's age under the Child Status Protection Act (CSPA) and current visa availability as early as possible to reduce the risk of the child aging out while the case is pending

Should we file for EB-5 if our child is nearly 21?

  • Families with children aged 18 to nearly 21 should assess EB-5 as early as possible
  • A child's age for immigration purposes depends not only on their date of birth but also on when Form I-526E is filed, how long the petition is pending, the Visa Bulletin when a visa becomes available, and whether the family seeks to acquire the visa within 1 year of it becoming available
  • These cases should be reviewed individually by an immigration attorney

What happens after Form I-526E is approved?

  • Once USCIS approves Form I-526E and a visa is available, the family typically proceeds through the National Visa Center (NVC): filing Form DS-260, preparing civil documents, completing the medical exam, and attending the interview at a U.S. consulate
  • Applicants already in the United States should have an attorney review their eligibility for adjustment of status under current policy

If I am already in the United States, can I apply for a green card there?

  • It depends on your current immigration status, when you file, and USCIS policy at the time your case is processed
  • A May 22, 2026 USCIS update indicates that adjustment of status (AOS) will be granted only in extraordinary circumstances
  • It should not be assumed that investors in the United States can always use concurrent filing — each case needs to be assessed by an immigration attorney

Once we receive EB-5 visas, does the whole family have to enter the United States together?

  • The principal applicant must enter the United States before or together with the derivative family members — derivative family members should not enter first
  • Everyone must enter before the expiration date printed on the visa (usually up to 6 months from issuance, and possibly sooner if the medical exam expires earlier)
  • You need to pay the USCIS Immigrant Fee before the physical green card can be issued (actual timing depends on USCIS and can take up to 90 days)
  • Families should keep the age rules in mind for any child not protected under the CSPA

What is the 2-year conditional EB-5 green card?

  • After entering the United States on an EB-5 visa or being granted permanent resident status, the investor typically receives a 2-year conditional green card
  • Before this green card expires, the investor must file Form I-829 to remove the conditions
  • Once Form I-829 is approved, the family receives unconditional permanent resident status

Do we have to live near the EB-5 project? How long must we stay in the United States after receiving green cards?

  • Living near the project site is not required for EB-5 investors — the family can live, study, and work in any U.S. state
  • Permanent residents must maintain the intent to reside permanently in the United States — an absence of more than 6 months may prompt additional questions at the port of entry
  • Staying outside the United States for more than 1 year, or beyond the validity of a reentry permit, may require a returning resident (SB-1) visa — and carries a risk of being found to have abandoned residence
  • Families should maintain a U.S. address, tax filings, bank accounts, insurance, school enrollment, and other genuine ties to the United States

How soon can I apply for U.S. citizenship?

Permanent residents can apply for U.S. naturalization after 5 years if they meet the following requirements:

  • Continuous residence
  • Sufficient physical presence
  • Good moral character
  • English and civics requirements

Extended travel can affect naturalization eligibility — families should plan their residence from the moment they receive their green cards.

Getting started with your EB-5 case

Do EB-5 green card holders have U.S. tax obligations?

  • U.S. permanent residents are generally treated as U.S. tax residents under the green card test, which means you may be required to file U.S. tax returns reporting your worldwide income, depending on your circumstances
  • This is an important tax and wealth consideration for investors with assets or businesses outside the United States
  • Consult a U.S. tax advisor both before and after receiving your green card

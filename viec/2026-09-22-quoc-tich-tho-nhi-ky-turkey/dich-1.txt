[s083] Turkey citizenship by investment FAQs
[s084] What are the investment risks?
[s085] Risks to keep in mind
[s086] The main risks sit with the real estate project itself.
[s087|#bo-qua-so: bỏ "(~10,4 tỷ đồng)" — chỉ nhắc lại số USD] For a property to qualify, it needs a valuation report of at least US$400,000, it must never have been used for a citizenship application before, and it cannot be bought from another foreign national.
[s088] On the citizenship side, risk is reduced when the file follows the process exactly: the program has no medical examination, no declaration of assets, and no restriction on the applicant's current nationality.
[s089] IMM Group prepares the file to the standard the government requires.
[s090] Note: real estate remains an investment, and its value can go down as well as up.
[s091] How is the investment structured?
[s092] A qualifying real estate purchase
[s093|#bo-qua-so: bỏ "(~ 10,4 tỷ đồng)" — chỉ nhắc lại số USD] For Turkish citizenship by investment you buy qualifying real estate worth US$400,000 or more, before taxes and fees.
[s094] After roughly 12–18 months of processing, depending on your file and the authorities, you, your spouse, and your unmarried, financially dependent children under 18 are granted Turkish citizenship.
[s095] How long must the property be held?
[s096] The holding period
[s097] Under the rules you hold the property for 3 years and give an undertaking not to sell before then; that undertaking is recorded on the Turkish land registry system.
[s098] During the holding period there is nothing to renew and no periodic filing to make.
[s099] After 3 years, IMM Group and our partners can help you resell the property without affecting your citizenship; family or friends can also buy it to file their own application.
[s100] If you would rather keep the asset and let it, you can sign a rental agreement with the developer.
[s101] What happens to my citizenship if the project fails?
[s102] Citizenship and project risk are two separate things
[s103] Once granted, citizenship is held for life and is not affected if the real estate project does not succeed: where the file met the requirements and the investment went into a qualifying project, you keep the citizenship you were granted.
[s104] The distinction matters, though: the property remains an investment that carries risk to both its value and the return of your capital — two separate questions.
[s105] What makes a property eligible?
[s106] Under the rules updated on March 22, 2021
[s107] Each title deed can be used for a citizenship application only once.
[s108] The property has to be bought from an individual or company of another nationality — for example from a Turkish company or individual.
[s109|#bo-qua-so: bỏ "(~10,4 tỷ đồng)" — chỉ nhắc lại số USD] The current threshold is US$400,000 or more, in force since the 2022 update. The rules that apply are those in force when you file.

[s132] Is there a US–Türkiye E-2 treaty?
[s133] The basis for the E-2 visa
[s134] The commercial treaty between the United States and Türkiye, which is the basis for the E-2 visa, dates from 1990 and has been in place ever since, with no fixed end date.
[s135] Any future change would depend on relations between the two countries and on what both governments agree.
[s136] Note: US policy and the conditions for granting an E-2 visa can still change, and the rules that apply are those in force when you file.
[s137] How well does the E-2 route work from Türkiye?
[s138] Türkiye and the E-2 route
[s139] Türkiye is one of the routes investors turn to most often when they are aiming at an E-2 visa through a second citizenship, because the investment level is reasonable, the requirements are simple, and processing is quick.
[s140] Türkiye holds a treaty with the United States covering both the E-1 and E-2 categories, and visas issued to Turkish nationals run for up to 60 months — longer than for many other countries. The E-2 route suits investors with a genuine business plan in the United States.
[s141] Note: the E-2 is a non-immigrant investor visa, not a green card or US citizenship.
[s142] Do I have to travel to Türkiye to obtain citizenship?
[s143] When you need to be in Türkiye
[s144] Under the current process you travel to Türkiye at least twice: once to file for your residence permit, and once to file your citizenship application and give biometrics.
[s145] Everything else can be handled under power of attorney from your home country.
[s146] Once you hold citizenship, your family is not required to live in Türkiye.
[s147] Do I need to live in Türkiye to apply for an E-2 visa?
[s148] The condition attached to treaty-country citizenship
[s149] To apply for an E-2 visa, someone who obtained Turkish citizenship through investment has to show a continuous 3-year connection with Türkiye under current US law. This is a legal test applied file by file, not simply a count of days spent in the country.
[s150] The exact conditions follow the E-2 rules in force when you file; check your position with an immigration attorney.
[s151] Up to what age can children be included?
[s152] Dependent children
[s153] The program allows unmarried children up to age 18 to be included in their parents' application.
[s154] Can parents be included?
[s155] Who counts as a dependent
[s156] Unfortunately not.
[s157] The program covers only the applicant, their spouse, and dependent children up to age 18 in one application.
[s158] If you need a route that covers parents as well, you could move to the Grenada citizenship program or a European program, which allow several generations in one application, or file a separate Turkish application for them.

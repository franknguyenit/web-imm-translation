## Domain notes for client

1. **Living abroad while holding a green card (s026, also s028/s042/s031).** A green card is permanent *residence*: holders are expected to make the United States their permanent home. Extended or repeated absences (especially 6 months or more, and presumptively 1 year or more without a reentry permit) can lead to a finding that status was abandoned, and absences also affect the continuous-residence requirement for naturalization. Green card holders are also generally U.S. tax residents on worldwide income. The line "many families continue to live and do business successfully at home after obtaining a U.S. green card" should be qualified or reviewed by counsel before publishing.
2. **Conditional green card.** EB-5 investors first receive a 2-year conditional green card and must file to remove conditions (Form I-829). The article speaks of "long-term residence" (s028) and "the journey to a green card" (s002) without this step; consider a short mention in the source.
3. **s002 sequence.** For applicants processing abroad, admission on the immigrant visa is when permanent residence begins — the green card is not a further "journey" after arrival. Source wording is imprecise; English kept it neutral.
4. **Children (s032, s040).** Children qualify as derivatives only if unmarried and under 21 (with Child Status Protection Act rules on "aging out"). Given the article's strong focus on children, the client may want to mention this.
5. **Total cost (s038).** The US$800,000 figure excludes administrative/project fees, legal fees, and government filing fees; readers assessing "surplus capital" may underestimate the total commitment.

[s001] Is EB-5 Worth It? Weighing the Real Trade-Offs of a U.S. Green Card
[s002] Months spent assembling a thick stack of documents. US$800,000 or more placed at real risk — capital that is invested and can genuinely be lost, not a one-off purchase price. Years of patient waiting for an immigrant visa before you can set foot in the United States and begin the journey to a green card.
[s003] Is that trade-off truly worth it?
[s004|#viet-nam: bỏ "gia đình Việt Nam" → families] In more than two decades as immigration advisors, this is the question the IMM Group team hears almost every week from families.
[s005] Our shortest and most candid answer: for the right person and the right family, EB-5 can be an extremely worthwhile decision.
[s006] That value does not come from seeing America as a perfect paradise, nor from any belief that a green card will make all of life's problems disappear. EB-5 truly delivers value only when it solves the problem your family is actually trying to solve: broadening your children's opportunities, spreading your assets and business across more than one place so you do not depend entirely on a single market, securing the right to live long-term in a major economy, and giving your whole family a reliable backup plan should the future bring unexpected change.
[s007] We want to be candid about this: IMM Group is an investment migration advisory firm. From a business standpoint, we naturally benefit when clients decide to proceed.
[s008] However, with more than 21 years in this profession, we know very well that persuading an unsuitable family to take this path leads to poor outcomes for everyone involved — the client, the advisor, and the firm's long-term reputation. This article, therefore, is not meant to make the decision for you or to persuade you through emotion. Our goal is to help you assess for yourself whether EB-5 is truly right for your family.
[s009] The greatest value is not the green card, but the "freedom to choose"
[s010] Many people see EB-5 as a simple transaction: pay a sum of money and receive a green card. That view is not wrong in form, but it is far too narrow in terms of value.
[s011] For a financially established family, the greatest value of EB-5 lies not in the plastic card itself, but in expanding the choices available for the whole family's future.
[s012|#viet-nam: "tại Việt Nam" → in your home country] You can continue to live, run your business, and build wealth in your home country, while having already prepared an international path for your children. You gain the right to place part of your life and business capital in a major market. And you give your children the freedom to choose where they study, work, and live in the future.
[s013] An option does not need to be used every day to have value. In risk management, some things are prepared not because we are sure we will need them soon, but because if a crisis ever comes, money alone cannot buy them on the spot.
[s014] That is the biggest difference between EB-5 and an ordinary purchase. Your family is not simply moving to a new home; you are preparing future generations to take part, over the long term, in an entirely different economic, educational, and legal system.
[s015] What makes the United States worth considering?
[s016] The United States has its share of shortcomings and realities that can disappoint newcomers. Yet, setting aside unrealistic, rose-tinted images, it still offers fundamental strengths that deserve a family's serious consideration:
[s017] 01. A society that values rules and protects people
[s018] The U.S. legal system can be complex, slow, and expensive. But when your rights are violated, there is always a clear path to file a complaint or bring the matter before an independent court. Your rights depend far less on "who you know." The clarity and protection of the law are an intangible asset of great long-term value.
[s019] 02. An education system that opens many paths for your children
[s020] The value of education lies not only in getting into a top school, but in the number of paths your children can choose. Whether they are suited to research, community college, vocational training, or an entirely new field, the U.S. system offers many starting points and many chances to change direction. Parents are not buying success for their children; they are opening doors so their children can choose freely.
[s021] 03. An economy large enough to allow a fresh start
[s022] A vast market and a clear division of labor make it easier for people to find work that matches their skills. American culture is open to career changes and to starting over after failure. A setback or two rarely becomes a "life sentence" for a career. This flexibility carries great value: the right to try, and the right to start again.
[s023] 04. Easy access to global markets
[s024] For business-owning families, having the legal right to live in the United States makes it easier to access capital, technology, and an enormous customer base. A large market always comes with fierce competition, but for capable and experienced people, it is an excellent platform for growing a business.
[s025] 05. Spreading your assets and your life across more than one place
[s026|#viet-nam: "Việt Nam" → your home country] Keeping all of your property, companies, income, and residence in a single country means placing your entire future in one place. Holding part of your assets in U.S. dollars and gaining the right to live in the United States can give your family greater peace of mind. This is not turning your back on your home country; in fact, many families continue to live and do business successfully at home after obtaining a U.S. green card. The right way to see it: you still believe in your home country, but you do not confine your children's entire future to a single geography.
[s027] 06. Green card benefits and long-term residence rights
[s028] A green card opens the way to long-term residence and the opportunity to apply for citizenship once you are eligible. Green card holders are free to travel, live in the United States, and enjoy legal protection of their rights. You do not have to stay in the U.S. 365 days a year. However, benefits always come with rules: from the outset, you should understand the requirements on the minimum time you must spend in the United States to maintain your green card, as well as the related paperwork.
[s029] A two-generation equation: parents make the sacrifices, children reap the rewards
[s030] One distinctive feature of immigration is that the people who bear the cost and the people who benefit are often two different generations.
[s031] The parents provide the capital, invest the time, prepare the finances, and accept disruption to their lives (including frequent travel between two countries). The children, meanwhile, are the ones who fully benefit from the education, language, friendships, and future career opportunities.
[s032] If you only calculate the short-term financial return for the person putting up the money, EB-5 may not be a profitable business proposition. But over a 20- to 30-year horizon, giving your children the right to live, study, and work in two countries is an invaluable long-term investment that parents make for their children.
[s033] The value of "freedom to choose" in an uncertain world
[s034] No one can know for certain how the world will change over the next 20 years. Economies may be volatile, technology may eliminate many old professions and create new ones, and your children's aspirations may change. In a world with so many variables, flexibility of choice is itself an asset.
[s035] Having an additional legal place to live does not mean leaving where you live now; it gives your family more room to adapt when circumstances change. Once you have built substantial wealth, the question is no longer "How do we make more money?" but "How do we use our money to bring freedom and security to our family?" Seen from that perspective, EB-5 is not a pure profit-seeking investment, but a way to exchange capital for long-term control over your options.
[s036] Which families are truly a good fit for EB-5?
[s037] The EB-5 program is only truly worth considering if your family meets all of the following conditions:
[s038] The investment of US$800,000 or more is long-term surplus capital that does not touch your business funds or your family's emergency savings.
[s039] Your family will still have enough cash on hand to maintain normal living and business operations after funding the EB-5 investment.
[s040] Your children have a clear desire to study or work in the United States over the long term.
[s041] The decision stems from your family's genuine needs, not from following a trend or from vague fears.
[s042] Family members have discussed in detail what it means to live apart, how parents on both sides of the family will be cared for, and the minimum time required in the United States to maintain the green card.
[s043] The decision-maker is looking at the picture 10 to 20 years ahead, rather than focusing only on when the capital will be returned.
[s044] On the other hand, your family should be very cautious if: the EB-5 investment makes up most of your current assets; you would need to borrow money to fund it; you and your spouse are not in agreement; or you are applying simply because others are doing it.
[s045] Facing the risks squarely to make a clear-headed decision
[s046] Talking at length about the benefits does not mean ignoring the risks. EB-5 capital is at risk and can be lost; processing times can be long; and project plans or immigration rules can change.
[s047|#viet-nam: "tại/ở Việt Nam" → at home, back home] Real life after moving to the United States is not automatically easy either. Housing, healthcare, and education costs are very high in some states. Someone with a strong social standing at home may well have to go through the experience of "starting over" in the United States. The pressure of caring for aging parents back home, along with family members living in different places, is also a major challenge.
[s048] These risks do not mean you should not pursue EB-5; they are a reminder that your family needs to prepare thoroughly. A good plan is not one without risk, but one that helps your family know exactly where the risks lie and have a response ready if things do not go as expected.
[s049] Counting the value that does not appear on a balance sheet
[s050] If you compare returns alone, EB-5 is certainly not the best investment for making money. Many other options are easier to execute, more liquid, and offer higher returns.
[s051] That is why EB-5 should not be judged as a purely financial investment. Other important forms of value to take into account include:
[s052] The right to live and work legally in a major country.
[s053] More education and career options for your children.
[s054] Building business and relationships across multiple countries.
[s055] A reliable plan for adjusting when your living environment changes.
[s056] The freedom to let your children choose where they want to live.
[s057] Peace of mind, knowing your family always has more than one path.
[s058] 10 questions to ask yourself before deciding
[s059|#bo-qua-so: "mười" viết chữ ở nguồn] Your family should sit down together and answer these 10 practical questions:
[s060] 1. If your investment is tied up for a long time or suffers a loss, will your family's finances still be secure?
[s061|#viet-nam: "tại Việt Nam" → at home] 2. After funding the application, will your company and your life at home still have enough cash to operate?
[s062] 3. Who in your family benefits most from this decision?
[s063] 4. Who will have to bear the most change and sacrifice?
[s072] One clear pattern in the history of the EB-5 program is that the minimum investment amount has risen over time (from US$500,000 to US$800,000), and it is expected to rise further.
[s073] That is why timing matters so much.
[s074] We always advise clients not to commit capital on impulse, out of fear of missing out, or under pressure from aggressive marketing.
[s075] That said, if your family has done its research, understands its needs, and is confident the program is the right fit, deciding early can bring substantial financial benefits. Acting sooner can allow you to lock in today's lower investment amount and may save you a considerable sum compared with waiting a few years and then having to invest a higher amount.
[s077] For a family with solid finances, capital that can be committed for the long term, clear goals for their children, and a desire to build an international foothold, EB-5 can be well worth it.
[s078] Its value reaches far beyond a green card. It lies in your children's future, in greater control over your business, in not concentrating all your risk in one place, and in the freedom for each family member to choose the life they want.
[s081] If your assessment points toward moving, begin with the most thorough preparation possible.
[s082|#viet-nam: "Việt Nam" → at home, home country] If it points toward staying, continue your life at home with peace of mind. Choosing your home country as your only base remains an entirely sound decision, and one that many families make with pride.

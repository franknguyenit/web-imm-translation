# Portugal D7 Visa

### What is the D7 visa?

- The Portugal D7 visa is a residence route for people with a steady passive income — with no investment to make and no business to run.
- One application covers the whole family: your spouse, children, and parents.
- You receive a residence permit that lets you live in Portugal and travel across the 29 Schengen countries on short stays under the 90/180-day rule.
- After 5 years of lawful residence, you may apply for permanent residence or for Portuguese citizenship if you meet the conditions set by Portuguese law.

## Benefits of the D7 visa

### Live in Portugal

- Your whole family can live, work, and study in Portugal.

### Visa-free travel in the Schengen Area

- Travel across the 29 Schengen countries under the standard short-stay rule: 90 days in any 180-day period.

### Healthcare and education

- Access to the public healthcare and education systems on the same terms as other residents.

### A path to citizenship

- After 5 years of lawful residence, and once you meet the residence, language, and integration conditions, you can apply for naturalization.

### Once you hold Portuguese citizenship

- Full EU citizenship rights: you can live, work, and study in any EU country.
- A strong passport: visa-free or eVisa access to more than 180 countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada.
- Portuguese citizenship, which follows continuous residence in Portugal, also opens access to the US E-2 investor visa, subject to the conditions of that program.

## Why applicants choose the D7

### No investment required

- All you have to show is a steady passive income — there is no capital to commit and no property to buy.

### Straightforward eligibility

- There is no language requirement, and no degree or management experience is needed.

### No age limit for applicants

- Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.

### Flexible rules for dependent children

- One of the few programs with no age cap on children, provided they are unmarried and financially dependent on you.

## 03 — Portugal D7 visa requirements

### Aged 18 or older, with no upper age limit.

- Meets security and background vetting requirements
- A steady passive income arising outside Portugal: rental income, financial investments, dividends, a pension, and similar.

### Meets security and background vetting requirements

- Children: unmarried and financially dependent on the applicant.
- Parents of both spouses: financially dependent on the applicant.

### Minimum passive income

- Applicant: from €10,440 a year.
- Each additional adult: a further €5,220 a year.
- Each child under 18, or other dependent: a further €3,132 a year.
- Funds for the first 12 months, held in a Portuguese bank account
- Applicant: at least €10,440; plus €5,220 per adult and €3,132 per dependent child.
- You need a residential address in Portugal, rented or owned, before you file.
- These figures are indicative, updated June 2026; the thresholds that apply are those in force when you file.

### Time you spend in Portugal

- While you hold the residence permit: do not spend more than 6 consecutive months, or 8 months in total over 2 years, outside Portugal.

## 04 — The Portugal D7 visa process

### Deposit and due diligence

Sign the deposit agreement and complete the initial due diligence on your file.

### Sign the contracts

Complete the formal service agreement.

### Prepare the file and the funds

Register for a Portuguese tax number and open a bank account in Portugal. Transfer the funds required as proof of means. Rent or buy a home to register your address.

### File the application

File your D7 visa application with the Portuguese embassy or consulate.

### Receive the visa and provide biometrics

You receive the D7 visa, valid for 4 months, and travel to Portugal to register for your residence permit with the immigration authority.

### Receive your residence permit

You collect the permit and move to Portugal. The first permit runs for 2 years and is renewed for 3 years at a time, as long as you continue to meet the conditions.

## Portugal D7 visa FAQs

#### What counts as passive income?

What counts as passive income

- It is steady income that does not come from employment in Portugal: rental income, dividends, returns on financial investments, a pension, and similar. The income has to arise outside Portugal and meet the minimum threshold set by the rules. The right evidence depends on your circumstances — contact IMM Group for advice on your own situation.

#### What is the minimum income?

The minimum income

- The applicant must show passive income of at least €10,440 a year, plus €5,220 a year for each additional adult and €3,132 a year for each dependent child. These figures are indicative at the time of writing and can change with the rules.

#### Do I have to invest or buy a home?

Whether you have to invest or buy a home

- No investment is required. You do need a residential address in Portugal before you file, which you can rent or buy as you prefer.

#### How many days a year must I spend in Portugal?

Days you must spend in Portugal

- While you hold the permit, do not spend more than 6 consecutive months, or 8 months in total over 2 years, outside Portugal. If you are aiming at naturalization later, you will also need to meet the residence and integration conditions set by law.

#### What is the path to citizenship?

A path to citizenship

- After 5 years of lawful residence you can be considered for permanent residence, or file for naturalization if you meet the residence, language, and integration conditions. Timing and outcome depend on your file and on the rules in force.

#### How do Schengen and the EU differ?

How Schengen and the EU differ

- The D7 permit allows short stays in the Schengen Area of 90 days in any 180-day period. The right to live and work in other EU countries arises only once you hold Portuguese, and therefore EU, citizenship.

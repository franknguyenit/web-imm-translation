Entrepreneur Immigration to Canada The SINP Entrepreneur Category

What was the SINP Entrepreneur Category?

  • The SINP Entrepreneur Category was designed for business owners and senior managers who wanted to invest in, establish or acquire a business in the province of Saskatchewan.
  • Investors had to run the business in Saskatchewan themselves to qualify for Canadian permanent residence.
  • A spouse and children under 22 could be included in the same application.
  • Former investment levels: from C$200,000 for a business outside Regina and Saskatoon, or C$300,000 for a business in either city.
  • Please note: the SINP Entrepreneur Category is closed to new applications. The Government of Saskatchewan announced that the Entrepreneur and Farm categories closed permanently on March 27, 2025, with no new applications accepted, no new Expressions of Interest (EOI) considered and no new Invitations to Apply (ITA) issued. This page is kept for reference; if Canada is still your goal, our advisors can point you to the Canadian programs that are a better fit today.

Benefits of the SINP Entrepreneur Category

Relocation for the whole family

  • The investor, spouse and dependent children could live, study and work in Saskatchewan for the duration of the work permit.

Education for your children in Canada

  • Children could attend public school free of charge through the end of high school. Once the family holds permanent residence, children can attend university at domestic tuition rates.

A route to permanent residence

  • Once the investment and business commitments were met and the province's conditions satisfied, the family could apply for Canadian permanent residence.

A path to Canadian citizenship

  • Once the family holds permanent residence and has met the residency, tax, language and background requirements, they can consider applying for Canadian citizenship.

Why investors chose this category

No education requirement

  • The category had no mandatory education requirement.

No English test at the application stage

  • No English language certificate was required at the initial application stage.

No security deposit

  • The province did not require a security deposit.

No age limit

  • There was no maximum age, although applicants aged 25–49 were given priority.

No same-industry experience required

  • Investors did not need experience in the same industry as the business they planned to run in Saskatchewan.

A clear job creation requirement

  • A business in Regina or Saskatoon had to create or maintain at least 2 jobs for Canadian citizens or permanent residents.

Applicant requirements

Business owner or senior manager

  • At least 3 years of business management experience within the previous 10 years.
  • A minimum net worth of C$500,000.
  • Clear security, background and medical checks.

Investment requirements

  • A minimum investment of C$300,000 for a business in Regina or Saskatoon.
  • A minimum investment of C$200,000 for a business outside Regina and Saskatoon.
  • A viable business plan that would deliver economic benefit to the province.
  • Hands-on, day-to-day management of the business in Saskatchewan.
  • Creation or maintenance of at least 2 jobs for Canadian citizens or permanent residents if the business was in Regina or Saskatoon.

The Saskatchewan application process

Assessment and case preparation

The applicant's eligibility, net worth, management experience and fit with the category were assessed.

Exploratory visit to Saskatchewan

The investor traveled to Saskatchewan to assess the business environment and prepare an investment plan.

Expression of Interest (EOI) submission

The EOI was submitted to the Government of Saskatchewan.

Invitation to Apply (ITA) and full application

Once invited, the investor completed the formal application for the province.

Provincial interview

The investor attended an interview with the Government of Saskatchewan.

Work permit application

Once approved by the province, the investor applied to the federal government for a work permit.

Move to Canada, invest and run the business

With the work permit issued, the investor moved to Saskatchewan, made the investment and ran the business in person.

Permanent residence application

After meeting the business and residency conditions, the investor applied for Canadian permanent residence.

Frequently asked questions about the SINP Entrepreneur Category

Is the SINP Entrepreneur Category still accepting new applications?

Is the SINP Entrepreneur Category still accepting new applications?

  • No. Saskatchewan's Entrepreneur and Farm categories closed permanently on March 27, 2025. The province no longer accepts new applications, considers new Expressions of Interest or issues new Invitations to Apply.

Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?

Is a work permit the same as permanent residence?

  • No. A work permit is temporary permission to live and work in Canada. The investor had to complete the investment, business operation and residency commitments before applying for permanent residence (PR). A work permit, permanent residence and citizenship are three separate stages and should not be treated as one.

Did the investor have to run the business personally?

Did the investor have to run the business personally?

  • Yes. The investor had to manage and operate the business in Saskatchewan in person. IMM Group and our partners can help with the business plan, but we cannot run the business on the investor's behalf.

Did the family have to live in Saskatchewan?

Did the family have to live in Saskatchewan?

  • Before permanent residence was granted, the investor and family had to remain established in the province they applied through, living and working there in line with the commitments they made to the category.

After permanent residence, can the family live in another province?

After permanent residence, can the family live in another province?

  • Once permanent residence is granted, the family can live and work in any province in Canada, like any other permanent resident.

What were the main risks of the category?

What were the main risks of the category?

  • The usual risks were a business plan that did not hold up under review, failure to meet the operating conditions, insufficient supporting evidence, or failure to comply with the commitments made to the province. Investing in a business and running it yourself is a commercial activity that carries risk.

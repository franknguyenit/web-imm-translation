[s004] The Portugal Golden Visa is a residency by investment program for investors who hold citizenship outside the European Union.
[s005] You and your family receive a Portuguese residence permit once you meet the program's investment requirement.
[s006] The whole family has access to healthcare, education, and social security in Portugal on the terms set by law.

[s008] After 10 years of holding the Golden Visa and meeting the investment and residence conditions, you can file for Portuguese citizenship.

[s080] What is a Golden Visa, and how does it differ from permanent residence?
[s081] What the Golden Visa is, and how it differs from permanent residence
[s082] The Portugal Golden Visa is a long-term residence permit for investors. In day-to-day terms it gives your family much of what permanent residence does: you can live, work, start a business, and put your children through school in Portugal.
[s083] The difference: the Golden Visa runs for a fixed term and has to be renewed periodically. After 10 years of residence, you can be considered for citizenship if you qualify.
[s084] What about Schengen and the EU?
[s085] Short stays in the Schengen Area — not a right to live or work elsewhere in the EU
[s086] The Golden Visa allows travel within the Schengen Area under the short-stay rules of 90 days in any 180-day period.
[s087] Travel to EU countries outside the Schengen Area still requires a visitor visa, though these are given priority handling.
[s088] The right to live, work, and study in any EU country arises only once you hold Portuguese citizenship.
[s089] Schengen is not the same as the EU
[s090] The EU — the European Union: an intergovernmental organization of 27 countries.
[s091] The Schengen Area is a zone of free movement between 29 member countries.
[s092] Some EU countries are not in Schengen, and the reverse is also true. Portugal is a member of both.
[s093] So with a Golden Visa you can already make short trips within the Schengen Area; the right to live and work across the EU arises only with citizenship.
[s094] Do my children study free of charge?
[s095] Free schooling for your children
[s096] While your family holds a valid Golden Visa, your children attend Portuguese state schools free of charge, from primary through the end of secondary school.
[s097] At university level, tuition and any support available depend on the institution and on your child's residence status at enrollment.
[s098] Is income earned outside Portugal taxed?
[s099] Tax on income earned outside Portugal
[s100] Portuguese income tax applies only once you become a tax resident — generally when you spend more than 183 days a year in Portugal.
[s101] Investors who visit only as long as they need to in order to keep or renew the permit usually fall outside that.
[s102] Tax policy can change, and your own position should follow tax advice at the time you file. (Updated: June 2026.)
[s103] What are my options after 5 years?
[s104] Your options after 5 years
[s105] After 5 years of holding the Golden Visa you have 3 options: keep renewing the residence permit; apply for permanent residence
[s106] If you keep renewing, you have to maintain the investment. If you apply for permanent residence or citizenship, once you qualify, you are no longer required to keep the investment.
[s107] What are the requirements for citizenship?
[s108] To be considered for Portuguese citizenship you need
[s109] To be 18 or older;
[s110] To have held a valid Golden Visa for at least 10 years, meeting the stay requirement of an average of 14 days every 2 years;
[s111] Portuguese at level A2;
[s112] And no criminal record in Portugal or abroad.
[s113] Applications are assessed under the rules in force when you file.
[s114] What is the A2 Portuguese exam?
[s115] The language requirement is a Portuguese certificate at CEFR level A2.
[s116] The exam can be run by an accredited school in Portugal or in another Portuguese-speaking country, by IEFP, or by an accredited test center.
[s117] A2 is a basic level; comparisons with IELTS are indicative only.
[s118] Is an online certificate accepted?
[s119] Online certificates
[s120] Under the rules, you and your family members need a certificate of completion from a Portuguese course at a state school in Portugal, or at a licensed center in Portugal.
[s121] Whether an online course is accepted depends on the rules in force — check before you enroll.

[s123] What citizenship gives you

[s136] How long is the permit valid, and how is it renewed?
[s137] Validity and renewal
[s138] The first Golden Visa permit runs for 2 years and can be renewed, usually for a further 3 years in the next cycle, provided you still hold the investment and have a clean criminal record.
[s139] After 5 years, if you qualify, you can apply for permanent residence. The validity and renewal cycles follow the rules in force at the time of renewal.
[s140] Is there an age limit for applicants?
[s141] The Portugal Golden Visa sets no upper age limit for the applicant.

[s143] Are qualifications or language skills required?

[s167] How many days a year must I spend in Portugal?
[s168] The Portugal Golden Visa does not require continuous residence.
[s169] To keep and renew the permit you need only meet the minimum stay requirement — an average of about 7 days a year, that is 14 days every 2 years.
[s170] If you plan to apply for citizenship later, you will also need to meet the residence, language, and integration conditions set by law.
